# Homework: Complex Arithmetic, Analytic Functions, and Contours

Give exact rectangular or polar forms as requested, and state orientations for
contour integrals.

## Problem 1 — Arithmetic and roots

Let $z=1-\sqrt{3}i$ and $w=-2+i$.

1. Compute $z+w$, $zw$, and $z/w$ in rectangular form.
2. Write $z$ in polar form with an argument in $[0,2\pi)$.
3. Find all cube roots of $z$ in polar form and sketch their locations.

## Problem 2 — Limits and complex derivatives

1. Decide whether $\lim_{z\to0}\overline z/z$ exists by comparing paths.
2. For $f(z)=z^3-2z+1$, compute $f'(z)$ and evaluate $f'(1+i)$.
3. Explain why two real partial derivatives at a point do not guarantee a
   complex derivative.

## Problem 3 — Cauchy–Riemann equations

For each function, write $u$ and $v$, solve the Cauchy–Riemann equations, and
state the set on which the function is analytic.

1. $f(z)=z^2+\overline z$.
2. $f(z)=|z|^2$.
3. $f(z)=e^x(\cos y+i\sin y)$.

## Problem 4 — Line segments and circles

1. Evaluate $\int_\gamma z^2\,dz$ along any path from $1$ to $1+i$ using a
   primitive.
2. Parameterize $\gamma(t)=t+it$, $0\le t\le1$, and evaluate
   $\int_\gamma\overline z\,dz$.
3. Evaluate $\oint_{|z|=2} (z^2+1)\,dz$ with positive orientation.
4. Evaluate $\oint_{|z|=2} dz/z$ and explain why Cauchy's theorem does not
   apply.

## Problem 5 — Challenge: a piecewise contour

Let $\gamma$ travel from $0$ to $1$, then to $1+i$, then to $i$, and finally
back to $0$.

1. Compute $\oint_\gamma \overline z\,dz$ by summing four line-segment
   integrals.
2. Compute $\oint_\gamma z\,dz$ and compare.
3. Explain geometrically why one integral detects the enclosed area while the
   other vanishes.
