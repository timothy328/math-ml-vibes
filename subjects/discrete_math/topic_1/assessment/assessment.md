# Discrete Math — Topic 1 Assessment

No solutions are provided for assessments.

## Problem 1 — Logic and proof

Let `a` and `b` be integers.

1. Formalize the claim: if `a+b` is odd, exactly one of `a` and `b` is odd.
2. Prove the claim.
3. State the contrapositive.
4. Give a counterexample to the claim “if `a+b` is even, both `a` and `b`
   are even.”

## Problem 2 — Sets and functions

Let `f: A -> B` and let `X,Y ⊆ B`.

1. Prove `f^{-1}(X ∩ Y)=f^{-1}(X) ∩ f^{-1}(Y)`.
2. Is `f(X ∩ Y)=f(X) ∩ f(Y)` always true? Prove or disprove.
3. Define injective and surjective.
4. Give a finite example of a function that is surjective but not injective.

## Problem 3 — Counting and probability

1. Derive the number of binary strings of length `n` with exactly three 1s.
2. How many length-8 strings have no adjacent 1s?
3. A classifier flags 12% of records, and 30% of flagged records are truly
   positive. If 5% of all records are truly positive, compute the probability
   a flagged record is truly positive and interpret it.
4. State the independence assumption used in your calculation.

## Problem 4 — Graphs and recurrence

1. Prove by induction that `1+3+5+...+(2n-1)=n^2`.
2. Define a recurrence for the number of nodes in a complete binary tree of
   height `h`, including the base case.
3. Solve the recurrence.
4. For a graph with 10 vertices and 15 edges, give an upper bound on the
   number of connected components and justify it.
